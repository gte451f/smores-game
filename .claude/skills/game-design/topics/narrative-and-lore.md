# Narrative and Lore

## Purpose

Worldbuilding and backstory: the deep history of the planet, how much of it a player ever
actually sees, and the in-fiction justification for why major systems behave the way they
do. Most of what's in this file is **background canon, not exposition** — it exists so the
design stays coherent and so any lore that *does* surface (environmental storytelling,
Codex entries per `player-experience.md`, ruins, dialogue) has a real history behind it,
not so it gets recited at the player. See Open Design Questions for how much of this is
ever actually delivered in-game.

## Worldbuilding Philosophy

A game is a reflection of what's possible, but it also has to be *plausible* — the default
approach is a coherent, in-fiction explanation for why things are the way they are, not an
arbitrary rule justified only by gameplay convenience. Some individual mechanics will still
ask the player to suspend disbelief (games always do), but that's the exception granted
deliberately, not the default posture.

**Tone: austere, stark, harsh.** This governs writing and art direction alike — the world
should read as depleted and unforgiving, consistent with the punishing tone already set in
`vision-and-pillars.md`.

## Threats

Three distinct sources of danger, all already present elsewhere in the design — this topic
just states that they're deliberately three *different kinds* of threat, not one danger
dial:

- **Intelligent hostile humanoids** — factions, bandits, rival survivors who plan, react,
  and hold territory (see `factions-and-world-state.md`).
- **Unintelligent predatory creatures** — wildlife that regards the squad as prey, reacting
  to proximity and hunger rather than strategy or standing (see `open-world.md`'s
  Wildlife).
- **The environment itself** — terrain, climate, and hazards that don't care about the
  player at all (see `open-world.md`'s Environmental Hazards and World Events).

## The Setting

### A World Terraformed, Then Abandoned

Long before the game's present day, an advanced precursor civilization targeted this
planet for terraforming. The work was never finished — it got far enough to produce a
genuinely breathable atmosphere and to seed plant life and animal life across all three
basic trophic roles (carnivore, herbivore, omnivore), but stopped short of full
completion.

### The Surface Colony and the Retreat Underground

A colony landed and built a real surface civilization on top of that partial terraforming.
Over time the atmosphere began thinning, exposing the surface to radiation — the
precursors themselves likely understood why, but that knowledge didn't survive to the
present day. Faced with that decline, the colony didn't fall all at once: some inhabitants
gradually abandoned the planet outright, while others retreated underground instead.

### The Karst Underworld

Those who went underground didn't just hide in caves — they built an extensive colony that
connected into a vast natural karst region beneath the surface. This underground world is
reserved for future DLC and isn't playable content today; it matters here purely as lore
that the present-day surface game is built on top of, and as the reason "underground"
means something specific and pre-existing rather than an arbitrary later addition.

### The Long Silence

Thousands of years (an approximate span, not a fixed date) passed after the retreat. Left
to itself, the surface world's seeded life kept evolving, shaped further by fluctuating
radiation levels — the present-day wildlife and vegetation are the mutated descendants of
what the precursors originally seeded, not new arrivals.

### Rediscovery, Unaware and Unconcerned

Much later, a future civilization — unrelated to the precursors and with no knowledge of
or interest in this history — stumbled on the planet and found two uses for it:

- **A penal colony.** Undesirable citizens are deposited here, their memories scrubbed,
  dropped onto the world with little or nothing to indicate their prior life.
- **A dumping ground.** The same arrangement doubles as a disposal site for waste and
  discarded material — the planet is, functionally, a place this civilization sends what it
  no longer wants, whether that's people or trash.

### The Prisoners' Civilization

The deposited prisoners didn't just survive — they built a real, primitive civilization
from what little they had, scraping out an existence in a harsh world using the alien
refuse dumped alongside them. They farm and ranch the land, contending with the mutated
creatures and plant life descended from the original precursor seeding, and fight off
predators to do it. This population, and its descendants, is the plausible root of the
present-day factions the player actually encounters (see `factions-and-world-state.md`).

## What This Explains

The point of writing all of the above down is that it should visibly answer "why" for
several already-decided systems, not just sit as unused flavor text:

- **Melee-primary combat** (`combat.md`) — a dumped prison population isn't handed
  firearms; advanced ranged weapons exist only as scarce, precious salvage, which is
  exactly why ranged combat is a supporting force multiplier rather than the default.
- **Structural faction weakness** (`factions-and-world-state.md`'s "Why the World
  Shouldn't Paint the Map") — a penal-colony population has no native industrial base to
  rebuild real militaries quickly, which is *why* low population and slow-recovering
  militaries are believable rather than an arbitrary balance knob.
- **No fast travel, static handcrafted map** (`open-world.md`, `world-map-and-travel.md`)
  — no orbital or high-tech transit infrastructure survived (or was ever given to
  prisoners); travel is genuinely ground-level.
- **Ruins and advancement resources** (`tech-and-crafting.md`, `economy.md`) — precursor
  ruins and salvage are the plausible source of the "specific dangerous or
  faction-contested locations" that gate advancement resources: reverse-engineered
  precursor tech, not homegrown invention.
- **The illicit and salvage economy** (`economy.md`) — a literal dumping-ground setting
  naturally supports a scavenging-and-fencing economy built on someone else's discarded
  material, on top of the ordinary illicit trades already designed.
- **A blank-slate starting squad** (`tutorial-and-scenario-start.md`) — the memory wipe is
  a direct, in-fiction reason a starter scenario's "prior life" stays vague and
  cosmetic-only, and why the player starts with next to nothing.

## Open Design Questions Worth Tracking

- Delivery mechanism(s) for how much of this ever actually surfaces in-game (environmental
  storytelling, ruins, Codex entries, dialogue) aren't decided — the working assumption is
  that most of it stays background canon the player pieces together partially, if at all,
  consistent with "not obvious to players."
- Exact timescale ("thousands of years") is approximate, not a fixed date.
- Whether present-day factions are entirely descended from the original prisoner
  population, or whether the depositing civilization keeps sending fresh "shipments" over
  time (mixing new arrivals with generations-settled descendants), isn't decided — this
  affects population variety and culture.
- Neither the precursor civilization nor the present-day depositing civilization has a
  name yet, and whether either ever appears directly (versus staying permanently offscreen)
  isn't decided — current lean is that the depositing civilization never appears at all.
- Whether the lineage roster (`characters-and-squads.md`) represents multiple origin
  worlds/species among the dumped "citizens," or is single-species, isn't resolved by this
  setting and needs its own pass.
- How much, if anything, the surface game should hint at the underground karst
  civilization (sealed entrances, rumors, precursor-era relics) before it becomes DLC
  content isn't decided.
- Relationship to Codex entries in `player-experience.md` — which pieces of this, if any,
  get a dedicated entry versus staying purely implicit.
